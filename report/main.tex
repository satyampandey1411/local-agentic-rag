\section{The problem and the goal}
Long PDFs such as research papers, government statistical releases and standards are hard to search.
Hosted chatbots can answer questions about them, but they cost money, send the document to a third party,
and often answer confidently when the PDF does not say. Our goal is an open-source chatbot that answers
questions about \emph{any} uploaded PDF under four constraints:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Local only.} Open-weight models and no API keys, so the document never leaves the runtime.
  \item \textbf{Free hardware.} One Colab T4: 15 GB GPU memory, 12.7 GB host RAM, 2 vCPUs.
  \item \textbf{Grounded.} Every answer cites the pages it used, or says the document does not contain the answer.
  \item \textbf{Multilingual.} Questions and documents may be in different languages, and scanned pages must work.
\end{itemize}

We started from the reference notebook and an early codebase that had only a non-agentic baseline
(MiniLM search, no tables, no OCR, English only). We rebuilt ingestion, retrieval and the agent, and measured
every change on a fixed benchmark.

\section{Approach and architecture}
The system has two paths. \textbf{Ingestion} runs once per PDF and is cached by file hash.
\textbf{Answering} runs per question. A single pass that retrieves, answers and verifies handles most questions,
and the agent loop starts only when that pass refuses or the verifier finds an unsupported claim.

{\fontsize{8.6}{10.4}\selectfont
\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{@{}>{\raggedright\arraybackslash\bfseries}p{36mm} >{\raggedright\arraybackslash}X >{\raggedright\arraybackslash}p{52mm}@{}}
\toprule
\color{navy}Decision & \color{navy}\textbf{Why} & \color{navy}\textbf{Measured effect} \\ \midrule
Hybrid search + cross-encoder reranker & Dense search finds paraphrases, BM25 finds exact labels and numbers, and the reranker reads question and passage together. & Page recall@5 73.1\% $\to$ 96.2\% (English models) \\
Table rebuilding & Statistical tables are aligned with spaces, so plain text extraction scrambles rows and columns. & Rows read as \texttt{label | 2025 Q2 = 3.8}. Table questions rose from 42\% to 83\%, helped by better retrieval and table captions \\
BGE-M3 + multilingual reranker & English-only models miss Hindi and Hinglish wording. & Multilingual set: recall@5 0.94 $\to$ 1.00 and MRR 0.88 $\to$ 0.97, with answer accuracy tied \\
Adaptive agent, not an always-on loop & Our first always-on loop was \emph{worse} than a single pass (23 vs 27 of 38): sub-queries crowded out the real evidence. & The loop runs only when needed, with fewer false refusals at similar accuracy \\
Python calculator & The 7B model subtracts and counts wrong even with the right numbers in view. & Arithmetic is exact and operands are checked against the cited row and column, but numerical questions are still 2/6 \\
Tesseract CLI OCR at 300 dpi & The built-in OCR path lost about 30\% of the Hindi words on scanned pages. & Hindi word recall 0.70 $\to$ 0.99 and 1.00 on the two scanned pages \\
Ollama for the LLM & Prebuilt CUDA binaries. Source-built alternatives failed on Colab's CUDA 13 and Python 3.13. & Installs in about 40 s on Colab, and the 7B model needs 5.2 GB \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}}

\subsection{Engineering for a free runtime}
The real limit was host RAM, not the GPU: the Ollama runner grew by about 100\,MB per request until Colab killed the session.
Memory-mapped weights, one request slot, retries and a watchdog that restarts the runner between requests fixed it. In the final run
the watchdog restarted the runner and no question failed. Parsed documents are cached by file hash, so re-runs skip parsing.

a U.S.\ BEA GDP release (28 pages, mostly statistical tables) and NIST AI 600-1 (64 pages). The questions cover seven categories,

and the English paper asked in Hindi and Hinglish. Final results: the project notebook run top to bottom on one T4 (on Kaggle, as Colab's free GPU quota was used up).

\section{Limitations}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Small benchmarks.} 61 English and 20 multilingual questions, written by one person. Grading matches required facts,
        which can miss a correct paraphrase. We report 95\% intervals for this reason.
  \item \textbf{Numerical questions} remain the weakest category. Retrieval finds the right page, but the 7B model often picks a number
        from the wrong row or column of a dense table.
  \item \textbf{Language coverage} is what BGE-M3 and Qwen2.5-7B handle well. We measured Hindi, Hinglish and Marathi only,
        on 20 questions. We do not claim support for other languages.
  \item \textbf{Not interpreted:} charts and figures. OCR quality depends on the scan.
\end{itemize}

\section{What we learned}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Retrieval moves accuracy the most.} Better search and reranking added 21 points of answer accuracy (50\% $\to$ 71\%).
        The agent loop and tools added a few more, mostly by refusing less often.
  \item \textbf{Measure on the real target.} The planning documents assumed CUDA 12 and Python 3.10 to 3.12, but Colab now has CUDA 13
        and Python 3.13. Host RAM, not the GPU, was the limit that crashed runs.
  \item \textbf{Every change needs a benchmark run.} A prompt rule that looked safe raised false refusals from 3.8\% to 21.2\%. Only the benchmark showed it.
\end{itemize}
